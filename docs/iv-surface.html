The volatility surface, measured

Implied vol inverted from 954,623 traded option closes across 112 underlyings — no assumed sigma, no assumed dividend yield. The forward comes from put–call parity, so the market tells us what it is rather than a model guessing.

The smile

Implied vol against distance from the forward. A flat line would mean Black–Scholes was right; it never is. The curve lifts on both wings and lifts harder on the put side — that asymmetry is the skew, and it is what makes downside protection expensive.

Steeper on short tenors: with days left, a 5% move is a tail event and gets priced like one. The near-dated wings are where everything the R1 test measured actually lives.

Term structure

At-the-money vol by days to expiry.

Surface grid

Mean IV by moneyness and tenor. Cold is cheap, hot is dear.

ATM vol against India VIX

India VIX is one ATM 30-day number. The surface reproduces it closely — which is the point: agreement validates the inversion, and everything VIX cannot say (skew, term, per-strike vol) is what the surface adds.

25-delta skew

Put IV minus call IV at 25 delta. Above the line, puts are richer than calls.

Method. Forward from put–call parity on the six strikes nearest the money (F = K + (C − P) / df), then Black–76 inverted by bisection. Dividends drop out entirely; the rate enters only through the discount factor.
Excluded by design. Expiry day (no time value), options at or below intrinsic (no root), and options within one tick of intrinsic — at a ₹0.05 tick that IV is quantisation noise, and it would land in exactly the deep-OTM region the research cares about.
